The diagnostic evidence for ARAGONITE over calcite is decisive: (1) the resolved nu4 doublet near 701/705 (calcite gives a single ~712 band), and (2) the strong lattice mode at ~213 cm-1 with a mode near ~156 (aragonite lattice pattern 152/206 vs calcite 156/282). The nu1 position of 1085.0 also sits at aragonite (1085) rather than calcite (1086) or dolomite (1097). Strong-band parameters are exceptionally well constrained (nu1 center to +/-0.008 cm-1). Weak lattice modes are poorly constrained: several eta values pin to 0 or bounds with uncertainties larger than the values, and peak_1 amplitude (96 +/- 67) and peak_2 amplitude (441 +/- 391) are barely significant. The nu1 core/base amplitude split is partially degenerate (base amplitude 2407 +/- 688). The persistent dispersive residual at the nu1 apex is physically meaningful: it suggests the real band is asymmetric, consistent with either instrumental/crystal-orientation effects or a genuine skewed lineshape not captured by symmetric pseudo-Voigts.

5. Caveats & Limitations

Identification is pattern-only: no elemental chemistry (EDX/XRF) or metadata was provided, so Ca is inferred from the carbonate fingerprint, not confirmed. A dispersive residual (~tens x noise) remains at the nu1 apex even in the 13-component model, indicating the symmetric core+base split does not fully capture an intrinsically asymmetric nu1 lineshape - amplitudes/areas of the nu1 core vs base are partially degenerate and should not be over-interpreted. Many weak lattice-mode parameters (peak_1, peak_2) are barely constrained (uncertainties comparable to values) and several eta values are pinned to bounds. The high-wavenumber pair (1206/1238) is assigned tentatively (combination/overtone region) and could include a minor organic or contaminant contribution. R2=0.99 reflects the strong bands and flat baseline and should not be read as validation of the nu1 apex fit.
