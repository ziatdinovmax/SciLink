Reference anatase modes: Eg ~144 (dominant), Eg ~197 (weak, often below detection; consistent with the very weak feature seen near 200), B1g ~399, A1g+B1g ~516 (unresolved doublet), Eg ~639, plus a weaker higher-frequency feature. The main Eg band FWHM (~8-21 cm^-1 depending on which component; the sharp core ~8.5 cm^-1) is broader than ideal single-crystal anatase, which can indicate nanocrystalline domain size or minor non-stoichiometry, though the shoulder-component ambiguity limits how firmly this can be stated. No chemistry (EDX/XRF) was provided, so this identification is pattern-based only.

5. Caveats & Limitations

Model-vs-data divergence: the fit uses SYMMETRIC pseudo-Voigt profiles, but the strong low-frequency phonons of anatase are intrinsically asymmetric. Two of the seven components (135 and 391 cm^-1, eta ~0, large amplitude errors) are fit-stabilizing shoulders, NOT independent modes, so band count (7) overstates the number of physical modes (effectively ~5). Residuals remain up to ~10x noise on the steep flanks of the 142 and 395 bands, confirming under-modeled asymmetry; reported FWHM of the strong bands is therefore biased and should not be used quantitatively for crystallite-size estimation without an asymmetric-profile refit. The characteristic weak anatase Eg mode near 197 cm^-1 is only marginally present. No elemental chemistry (EDX/XRF) was provided, so the identification is pattern-only and cannot exclude minor secondary phases or dopants. The weak 795 cm^-1 band is broad, poorly constrained (center error ~1.4 cm^-1), and may be an overtone/combination or a fit artifact.
