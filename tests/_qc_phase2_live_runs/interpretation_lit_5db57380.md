## What narrow, slightly right-skewed edge-length distributions imply (mechanism and uniformity)
### 1) Narrow distributions imply strong kinetic focusing and/or post-synthetic selection
A narrow edge-length distribution (e.g., relative SD of only a few percent, as in 128 ± 6 nm) is consistent with growth conditions that suppress continued nucleation and enforce similar growth histories across seeds (moal2016engineeringtheemission pages 2-3). For plate-like fcc nanocrystals, a central control variable is maintaining **low precursor supersaturation during growth**: when supersaturation is high, the nucleation barrier can be overcome on many facets, enabling **simultaneous in-plane and out-of-plane growth** and producing “smaller and thicker particles with reduced 2D anisotropy” (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 5-6). Conversely, protocols that **add precursors dropwise or in sequential steps** are explicitly motivated as a way to maintain low supersaturation and thereby sustain selectivity for side-facet (lateral) growth (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 5-6). Such controlled monomer supply is a classic route to size focusing (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 5-6).

Additionally, narrow distributions can reflect **post-synthetic purification/selection**, not only intrinsic growth. For example, Kim et al. explicitly remove “small gold polyhedral particles” and “large plate-like impurities” by a multi-round depletion-attraction purification in concentrated CTAC (kim2022symmetrybreakinginpatch pages 9-9). Such selective removal will narrow the apparent distribution even if the crude product is broader (kim2022symmetrybreakinginpatch pages 9-9).

### 2) Right-skew (a longer tail to larger edge lengths) suggests a minority population with extra growth time or faster growth rates
Although skewness could not be directly verified from histograms in the accessible corpus (image retrieval failed; only mean ± SD could be confirmed), a **slightly right-skewed** edge-length distribution is commonly interpreted as the superposition of a dominant, well-focused population plus a small subpopulation that becomes larger.

Mechanistically, for plate-like particles this can arise from:
- **Late-forming seeds / secondary nucleation** that subsequently “catch up” imperfectly, or from a small fraction of seeds experiencing earlier nucleation (more time to grow), yielding a high-size tail.
- **Heterogeneity in seed crystallography/defect content**, since the plate-like morphology is tied to twinning/stacking faults in seeds (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 3-4, scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 5-6). A subpopulation of seeds with defect structures that promote faster or longer-lived lateral growth could populate the right tail.
- **Incomplete removal of large plates** during purification: if purification preferentially removes only the most extreme large plates, residual larger triangles can remain as a right tail (consistent with the need to remove “large plate-like impurities” during purification workflows) (kim2022symmetrybreakinginpatch pages 9-9).

### 3) Narrowness + mild right-skew together imply “high uniformity with small outliers,” not perfect monodispersity
Using the user-provided summary statistics (mean ~101 nm; SD ~7.7 nm), the coefficient of variation is ~7.6%, which indicates good but not “atomically monodisperse” uniformity. In practical terms this typically corresponds to:
- Tight optical resonance distributions and reproducible plasmonic behavior for most particles, with a minor tail that can broaden ensemble spectra.
- A growth process that is largely controlled (low supersaturation; facet-selective adsorption) but not perfectly self-limiting.

### 4) Literature-supported mechanistic levers that produce (or explain) such uniformity
Mechanistic themes for controlling nanoplate size/shape distributions emphasized in the nanoplate synthesis literature include:
- **Seed defect control**: controlling twin-defect populations in seeds can improve “yield and size dispersity” (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 5-6). 
- **Oxidative etching and defect/seed selection**: “enriched planar twinned seeds” can be prepared by careful oxidation of presynthesized/purified Au nanotriangles, and better-defined seeds are a path toward improved control (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 5-6). 
- **Facet-selective adsorption and halides**: ligand/halide binding (e.g., citrate, iodide) modulates facet growth and can promote/stabilize platelet habits (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 16-17, scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 5-6). 
- **Oxidative etching as a focusing tool**: controlled oxidative etching (e.g., I−/O2 pairs) is highlighted as enabling impressively narrow size dispersion in related 2D systems (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 16-17).

## Literature-Refined Interpretation

## Revised interpretation

The analysis segments a field of touching/overlapping triangular gold nanoparticles imaged by TEM at a resolved pixel size of 0.355 nm/px. Of 31 detected objects, 28 fully-interior particles were quantified while 3 edge-truncated particles were (correctly) excluded from statistics. The extracted per-particle areas were converted to an 'equivalent edge length' assuming an equilateral triangle (A = (sqrt(3)/4)*L^2), which is the physically meaningful size metric for these plate-like triangular prisms grown by anisotropic (facet-selective) colloidal synthesis. This morphology assignment is consistent with the literature: triangular Au nanoplates/nanoprisms in this size regime are well documented (e.g., Le Moal 2016 reports gold nanotriangles at 128 ± 6 nm edge length; Kim 2022 reports triangular Au nanoprisms), and their plate-like habit is understood to arise from twinning/stacking-fault-containing seeds that promote lateral (side-facet) growth under low precursor supersaturation (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 3-4, pages 5-6). Note, however, that these particles are genuine plates with finite thickness — reported thicknesses are substantial (e.g., 69 ± 7 nm for the 128 nm-edge triangles of Le Moal 2016; 27.2 ± 3.7 nm for the 62.5 nm-edge prisms of Kim 2022) — so the projected TEM area we measure is the in-plane footprint, and the "equivalent edge length" is strictly a 2D lateral size metric, not a full 3D descriptor.

The size distribution is tight and unimodal: median edge 100.4 nm, mean 100.9 nm, std 7.7 nm (CV ~7.7%), spanning ~89–128 nm. This narrow, single-peaked distribution is the key physical result: it indicates a highly monodisperse population. The measured CV of ~7.7% is comparable to, though slightly broader than, published relative SDs for triangular Au nanoprisms in nearby size windows (~4.7% for Le Moal 2016; ~6.2% for Kim 2022) (moal2016engineeringtheemission pages 2-3, kim2022symmetrybreakinginpatch pages 2-3), so "highly monodisperse" should be stated as "good uniformity with small outliers" rather than perfect monodispersity. Such narrow distributions are literature-consistent with growth conditions that suppress continued nucleation and enforce similar growth histories across seeds — in particular, maintaining low precursor supersaturation to sustain selectivity for lateral facet growth (often via dropwise/sequential precursor addition), which is a classic route to size focusing (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 5-6). Importantly, the literature adds a mechanism the raw statistics alone cannot distinguish: a narrow apparent distribution can also reflect post-synthetic purification/selection (e.g., depletion-attraction purification that removes small polyhedra and large plate impurities), not only intrinsic kinetic focusing (kim2022symmetrybreakinginpatch pages 9-9). We therefore cannot infer purely from these data whether the tightness is intrinsic to growth or partly the result of size selection.

Monodispersity of edge length matters because the localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) of triangular Au nanoprisms is governed primarily by edge length and tip sharpness; a ~8% edge-length spread implies a correspondingly narrow, well-defined plasmonic response — desirable for sensing, SERS, and photothermal applications. Consistent with the literature, most of the population would produce a tight, reproducible optical resonance, with the minor high-size tail contributing some ensemble spectral broadening (moal2016engineeringtheemission pages 2-3).

The absence of a low-value fragment population (no cluster of areas below ~1/4 of the ~4360 nm² median, i.e. no spike near ~1000 nm²) argues against over-segmentation: SAM split the touching triangles into physically plausible whole objects rather than shards.

The distribution is mildly right-skewed: a dominant, well-focused mode near ~100 nm with a longer tail toward larger edge lengths, culminating in one clear outlier at 7086 nm² (~128 nm 'edge') that sits far from the main mode. Two readings of this tail are now warranted. First, as originally noted, the outlier may be a segmentation artifact — a merged mask of two overlapping triangles — in which case the true upper tail is closer to ~112 nm. Second, the literature offers a genuine physical origin for a right-skewed tail that should not be dismissed: a minority subpopulation with extra growth time or faster growth (from late-forming or secondary-nucleated seeds, from seed crystallographic/defect heterogeneity that promotes faster or longer-lived lateral growth, or from incomplete removal of large plates during purification) (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 3-4, pages 5-6, kim2022symmetrybreakinginpatch pages 9-9). Notably, the single largest particle here (~128 nm edge) coincides almost exactly with the real, synthesized nanotriangle size reported by Le Moal 2016 (128 ± 6 nm), so a value in this range is physically plausible for a true triangle and not necessarily a merged artifact. The image context — the dense, very-dark cluster in the lower-center/left that may harbor heavily overlapping prisms — keeps the artifact interpretation viable, but the two hypotheses cannot be separated from area statistics alone. We flag the outlier as ambiguous: probable merged mask, but possibly a real large-tail particle. Correspondingly, the true count remains slightly uncertain (likely within the recommended ~±20%) due to possible merging/under-detection in the darkest regions.

Net interpretation: a well-controlled, good-uniformity (CV ~7.7%) ensemble of plate-like triangular Au nanoprisms with lateral edge ~100 nm, consistent in shape, size regime, and distribution narrowness with published seed-mediated, low-supersaturation, facet-selective syntheses of triangular gold nanoplates (scarabelli2023platelikecolloidalmetal pages 5-6, moal2016engineeringtheemission pages 2-3, kim2022symmetrybreakinginpatch pages 2-3, pages 9-9). The tight unimodal core is robust; the mild right-skew is genuine and may reflect either a real minority large-particle subpopulation (secondary nucleation, seed-defect heterogeneity, or incomplete large-plate removal) or a small number of segmentation merges confined to the most overlapped/darkest regions. The tightness itself may be intrinsic (kinetic focusing) and/or the result of size-selective purification — the data cannot distinguish these, and thickness is not measurable from these projections.
