### Objective and Approach The goal of this analysis was to detect a gold (Au) coupon within a 20 × 20 mm field of view and to estimate its thickness using the Au K-edge—a sharp, element-specific jump in X-ray absorption that occurs at 80.7 keV. Because gold has a very high atomic number, photons just above this energy are absorbed far more strongly than those just below it. The size of this absorption 'step' at each pixel is proportional to how much gold the X-rays passed through, making it a robust fingerprint for confirming the presence of the metal and estimating its thickness from the population statistics. A critical methodological point governs the reliability of these results. The sample coupon fills essentially the entire imaged area, so there is no empty 'no-sample' region within the primary image to serve as a reference beam. The analysis therefore correctly relied on the separately supplied flat-field (I0) reference cube—an image of the X-ray beam with no sample present—to compute the true absorption at every pixel. This is the physically correct procedure; attempting to derive a reference from the sample image itself would have artificially suppressed the very signal we are trying to measure.
