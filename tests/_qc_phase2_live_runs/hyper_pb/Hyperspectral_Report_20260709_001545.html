1. Synthesized Scientific Analysis

### Objective and Approach The goal of this analysis was to locate a lead (Pb) coupon within the imaged field and to measure its thickness using the characteristic Pb K-edge absorption discontinuity at 88.0 keV. Because Pb has a sharp, element-specific jump in X-ray attenuation at this energy, the size of that jump in each pixel provides, in principle, a direct fingerprint of how much Pb the X-ray beam passed through. This makes the K-edge subtraction method the physically appropriate tool for the task, and it is the accepted standard for element-specific thickness measurement in transmission X-ray imaging. ### Data Treatment and Model Rather than relying on exploratory statistical decomposition (which captures variance patterns but does not directly yield physical thickness), the analysis used a physics-based, custom-coded mapping. For each pixel, the optical depth (the natural logarithm of the ratio of the incident beam to the transmitted beam) was computed just below and just above 88.0 keV using narrow energy windows. The difference between these two values is the K-edge step, which is intended to isolate the Pb signal and cancel out smooth, non-Pb backgrounds such as beam hardening. Low energies (below ~40 keV) were excluded because in a 160 kVp bremsstrahlung beam they are dominated by scatter and noise. A companion open-beam (no-sample) reference cube was available on the same measurement grid and served as the incident-beam baseline. The measured edge step was then converted to a nominal thickness using the tabulated Pb attenuation jump (mass-attenuation jump of 5.423 cm²/g multiplied by the Pb density of 11.35 g/cm³, giving an expected attenuation-jump of 61.55 per cm). ### Findings **The spatial maps are dominated by pixel-level speckle, not coherent spatial structure.** Both the edge-jump map and the derived thickness map display a high-frequency, salt-and-pepper texture with no spatially-correlated features: there is no coupon boundary, no gradient, and no core/shell/edge structure anywhere in the 80×80 field. A genuinely uniform physical foil would be expected to produce a smooth, spatially-correlated map rather than the random pixel-to-pixel variation observed here. The maps are therefore consistent with a noise- or background-dominated differential measurement, and any spatial-coherence filtering that may have been intended was not effective at suppressing this speckle. **The value distributions are single-peaked but do not, by themselves, establish genuine Pb detection.** The edge-jump values cluster around a mean of approximately 0.48 optical-depth units with a spread of ~0.06, and the corresponding thickness values center near 0.08 mm (std ~0.01 mm). While the histograms are clean and near-Gaussian, a tight Gaussian around a nonzero mean is fully consistent with a noise- or background-dominated differential measurement and cannot, on its own, distinguish signal from noise. In the absence of any resolved spatial structure, the histogram shape should not be read as confirmation of a well-resolved physical Pb object. **Absolute Pb presence and thickness cannot be robustly established from these data.** There is no in-frame open-beam region and no visible coupon boundary, so the analysis cannot distinguish a real "Pb everywhere" scenario from a global background or normalization offset applied uniformly across the field. Any nominal thickness figure (nominally ~0.08 mm / tens of micrometers) inherits this ambiguity and the noise-dominated character of the maps, and should be treated as an unverified estimate rather than a measured value. ### Confidence and Caveats Given that the spatial maps are dominated by salt-and-pepper noise with no coherent structure, confidence in both Pb detection and thickness quantification is low. The single-peaked histograms do not resolve this: distribution shape alone cannot separate signal from background here. The lack of a true in-frame open beam prevents disambiguation of Pb presence from a uniform normalization/background offset, and the nominal ~0.08 mm thickness therefore should not be reported as a reliable quantity even at the tens-of-micrometers level. No claim can be made about foil homogeneity or the absence of defects: because the maps are themselves pixel-level noise, real thin-foil features such as pinholes, cracks, or local thinning would be indistinguishable from the pervasive speckle.
