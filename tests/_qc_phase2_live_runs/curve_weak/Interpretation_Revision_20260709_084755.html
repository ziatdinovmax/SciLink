## Revised interpretation

The spectrum is confidently assigned to **ARAGONITE (orthorhombic CaCO3)**. This assignment is directly supported by the feature-conditioned literature: a study distinguishing carbonate polymorphs reports aragonite diagnostic peaks at ~1082, ~702, and ~285 cm−1, versus calcite at ~1084, ~710, and ~282 cm−1 (grenier2024microstructurescrystallographyand pages 4-5). The two most diagnostic discriminators in this spectrum both match aragonite: the ν4 in-plane bend near **701–705 cm−1** aligns with aragonite's ~702 cm−1 and is notably below calcite's characteristic ν4 at ~710–713 cm−1 (kim2021identificationandcomposition pages 2-3, 3-5; grenier2024microstructurescrystallographyand pages 4-5), and the lattice mode at **283.7 cm−1** matches aragonite's ~285 cm−1 rather than calcite's ~279–282 cm−1 (kim2021identificationandcomposition pages 2-3, 3-5; grenier2024microstructurescrystallographyand pages 4-5).

Band assignments against carbonate group frequencies: the dominant band at 1085.4 cm−1 (peak_8, rel. intensity 1.0) is the ν1 symmetric C-O stretch. Its position near ~1085 cm−1 is common to both CaCO3 polymorphs and is therefore not by itself discriminating (kim2021identificationandcomposition pages 2-3, 3-5; grenier2024microstructurescrystallographyand pages 4-5); the polymorph call rests on the ν4 and lattice-mode positions above.

The resolved doublet at 701.3 and 705.6 cm−1 (peaks 6 and 7) is the ν4 in-plane bend. Its centroid matches aragonite's ~702 cm−1, and its position below calcite's single ~710–713 cm−1 ν4 is a decisive discriminator from calcite (kim2021identificationandcomposition pages 2-3, 3-5; grenier2024microstructurescrystallographyand pages 4-5). Note that the cited literature reports a single aragonite ν4 near ~702 cm−1 and does not itself document the fine splitting into 701/705 cm−1; the resolved doublet here is a data-supported observation consistent with aragonite's site-symmetry lifting of degeneracy, but the specific two-component split is not independently corroborated by the retrieved sources.

The lattice/external modes at 142.8, 189.5, and 213.4 cm−1 (peaks 1, 11, 3) correspond to aragonite's translational/librational lattice modes, with 213 being the strong lattice mode (peak_3, rel. intensity 0.079). The 272/284 cm−1 pair (peaks 4, 5) are additional lattice modes; the 283.7 cm−1 component in particular matches aragonite's ~285 cm−1 diagnostic band and lies above calcite's ~279–282 cm−1 lattice mode (grenier2024microstructurescrystallographyand pages 4-5; kim2021identificationandcomposition pages 2-3, 3-5). The retrieved sources did not provide cited assignments sufficient to use the 142/213/272 cm−1 bands as independent polymorph discriminators, so their assignment to aragonite lattice modes is by consistency rather than direct literature match; this does not weaken the identification, which is carried by the ν4 and ~284 cm−1 features. It is worth flagging that ~154–156 cm−1 is reported as a characteristic calcite lattice mode (kim2021identificationandcomposition pages 2-3, 3-5); the marginal peak_2 (~155, see below) sits at this position, but given its low significance and the strongly aragonite-favoring ν4/~284 evidence, it does not support a calcite (co-)assignment.

The weaker higher-shift features at ~1111 (peak_9) and the 1206/1235 bands (peaks 10, 12) fall in the region of ν3 antisymmetric stretch / combination and overtone territory (bands near 1200–1240 can include weak ν1+lattice combinations); these are consistent with carbonate but are weak and less individually diagnostic. The retrieved literature did not provide a cited assignment for a ~1206 cm−1 band in either calcite or aragonite, so this feature is neither corroborated nor contradicted by the available evidence.

Every fitted component corresponds to visible intensity — no components are parked on noise, except peak_2 (~155) and peak_9 (~1111) which are marginal (large fractional errors, degenerate eta). The baseline is genuinely near-flat (baseline_fraction ~0.11, offset ~0), so fluorescence is negligible and band intensities are trustworthy.

The dominant ν1 band is asymmetric (fitted fwhm_left 4.47 > fwhm_right 2.31, asymmetry_ratio 0.52), sharper on the high-shift side; however the persistent dispersive apex residual (~±1000 counts localized at 1083.5) means the exact peak shape and thus the reported FWHM (~3.4 cm−1) and asymmetry are not fully resolved and should be treated as approximate.

Overall fit quality R2=0.996, RMSE=74 counts against a 22000-count peak. In summary, the literature explicitly identifies this feature set — a dominant ν1 near ~1085 cm−1 with ν4 near ~702 cm−1 and a lattice mode near ~285 cm−1 — as aragonite rather than calcite (grenier2024microstructurescrystallographyand pages 4-5; kim2021identificationandcomposition pages 2-3, 3-5), corroborating the assignment.
