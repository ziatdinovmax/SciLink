5. Caveats & Limitations

The disclosed model matches the data with no divergence, so interpretation is well-supported. However: (1) the constant baseline C is statistically zero (2.3e-10 +/- 3.7e-3) and carries no physical meaning — it should not be interpreted as a real background floor. (2) The physical assignment of each lifetime to a specific mechanism (radiative vs nonradiative, surface vs bulk, distinct populations) is not determined by the fit alone and requires independent corroboration. (3) tau2 has ~6% uncertainty because it lives in the low-signal, noisier tail; a longer acquisition window or higher tail counts would tighten it. (4) Absolute amplitudes depend on instrument scaling and are only meaningful as ratios/fractions. (5) Any instrument response function (IRF) convolution at very short times was not evident in this fit; if the fast lifetime approached the IRF width it could be biased, though 60 ns is typically well above IRF timescales.
