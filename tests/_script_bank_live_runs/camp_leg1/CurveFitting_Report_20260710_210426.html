In this in-situ context a moving lattice is the point of the experiment, not a fitting artifact. The eta rail-pinning (many peaks at eta~0 pure Gaussian or eta~1 pure Lorentzian) indicates the lineshape mixing is under-constrained for individual weaker peaks and should not be over-interpreted per-peak, though the aggregate fit quality is unaffected.

5. Caveats & Limitations

The disclosed model matched the Stage 1 hypothesis, so no model-vs-data divergence penalizes the interpretation; however: (1) The Williamson-Hall size+strain decomposition is invalid (R2=0.008, negative slope) — per domain rules only a per-peak Scherrer size RANGE is reported and NO strain value is quoted. (2) Per-peak sizes vary >3x, indicating anisotropic broadening; the isotropic ~23 nm mean is an oversimplification. (3) No instrumental-FWHM subtraction is evidenced, so quoted sizes are lower bounds unless the instrumental broadening (synchrotron, typically very small) is confirmed negligible; at synchrotron resolution this is usually acceptable but should be verified. (4) Many eta values pin at 0 or 1, meaning Gaussian-Lorentzian mixing is unconstrained for individual (especially weak) peaks — do not interpret per-peak lineshape character. (5) peak_2 (~6.1 deg, FWHM 1.6 deg, Scherrer ~5 nm) is likely a broad diffuse/background feature, not a true Bragg reflection, and should be excluded from size statistics. (6) Localized derivative residuals at the sharpest apexes (11, 22, 26 deg) are the accepted split-PV profile limit, not unmodelled peaks. (7) This is one frame of an in-situ ramp; positions/widths/intensities evolve with dehydration state and the interpretation applies only to this frame.
