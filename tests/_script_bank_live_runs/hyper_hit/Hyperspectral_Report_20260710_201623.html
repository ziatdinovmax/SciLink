### Objective and Approach The goal of this analysis was to locate a bismuth (Bi) coupon within a spectral X-ray transmission dataset and to measure its thickness using the characteristic Bi K-edge absorption discontinuity near 90.5 keV. Because the fundamental measurement here is a sharp, element-specific step in attenuation rather than a set of overlapping spectral phases, a physics-based custom mapping approach was prioritized over generic statistical decomposition. This is the correct methodological choice: the accepted standard for foil thickness determination is the differential K-edge (dual-energy) method, which measures the jump in optical depth across the edge and cancels smooth, slowly varying backgrounds such as beam hardening and detector-response effects that would otherwise bias a full-spectrum fit. ### Data Conditioning and Method For every pixel, the measured transmission was normalized against a companion flat-field reference (the baseline_I0 datacube) to form I/I0, and the optical depth ln(I0/I) was evaluated in narrow, robust continuum windows placed just below and just above the Bi K-edge. The difference between the above-edge and below-edge optical depths yields the edge jump, Δ(μt), which serves as an element-specific proxy for the aggregate areal amount of Bi along each ray. Low-energy channels below roughly 40 keV — which in a 160 kVp bremsstrahlung beam are dominated by scatter and noise for high-Z materials — were deliberately excluded, consistent with best practice. The top-border strip is consistent with detector-edge artifacts; the sparse interior rejected pixels are consistent with the same per-pixel noise process that dominates the rest of the map (see below) and should not be over-interpreted as physical gaps. A significant limitation must be stated plainly: there is no bare-beam (open-beam) reference region anywhere inside the frame. As a result, the data contain no spatial contrast and no negative control. The measurement therefore cannot distinguish between the interpretation that 'Bi fills the entire field of view' and the alternative that a uniform absorbing background or a systematic offset is present across the detector.
