1. Synthesized Scientific Analysis

### Objective and Approach The goal of this study was to detect a gold (Au) signature within a spectral X-ray transmission image and to quantify its thickness. The measurement leverages a fundamental physical signature: the Au K-edge, a sharp discontinuity in X-ray absorption that occurs at ~80.7 keV when incident photon energy becomes sufficient to eject electrons from the innermost (K-shell) atomic orbital of gold. Because this edge is element-specific, its magnitude directly encodes how much gold the X-ray beam passed through, independent of smooth background absorption from other materials. ### Method: Element-Specific Edge-Jump Mapping Rather than relying on a general statistical decomposition (which would be dominated by smoothly varying backgrounds, beam-hardening effects, and low-energy scatter), the analysis applied a physics-based, per-pixel measurement. For each pixel, the transmitted intensity was normalized against a companion flat-field (I0) reference to form a transmission ratio, and the absorption (optical depth, ln(I0/I)) was measured in narrow energy windows immediately above and below the Au K-edge. The difference between these two windows yields the K-edge jump, Δ(μt) — the quantity that is directly proportional to the amount of gold along the beam path. This differential approach is the accepted standard because it cancels out smooth, non-gold contributions to absorption. Low-energy regions (below ~40 keV), which are known to be dominated by scatter and noise in high-voltage bremsstrahlung imaging, were excluded, and pixels with insufficient signal-to-noise were masked out. ### Field-Averaged Signal and Thickness The Edge_Jump map shows a gold signature present across essentially the entire imaged field, with a tight statistical distribution centered near Δ(μt) ≈ 0.48 (standard deviation ~0.05). Importantly, the spatial map is dominated by pixel-to-pixel salt-and-pepper texture with no discernible spatial structure: no coupon boundary, edge, or shape is resolved anywhere in the field of view. This means the analysis can establish a consistent field-averaged gold signal, but it cannot spatially localize a distinct coupon or distinguish a coupon from a full-field uniform attenuator. The narrowness of the histogram indicates only that the mean signal level is consistent across pixels; it does not, on its own, demonstrate spatial homogeneity, because the per-pixel measurement is noise-limited. The few blue border pixels and scattered interior points correspond to edge-of-detector effects and isolated low-signal pixels.
