Interpretation

The pattern is fit as 32 split pseudo-Voigt reflections on a SNIP background over 5-45 deg, achieving global R^2 = 0.9999996 and peak_region_R^2 = 0.9997. The strong, isolated reflections at 11.93 deg (amplitude ~1.27e6, area ~3.47e5) and 19.99 deg (amplitude ~1.02e6, area ~2.93e5) dominate the pattern, with secondary strong features at 15.95 and 17.89 deg. FWHMs of the well-defined mid-angle reflections cluster around 0.25-0.31 deg, corresponding to microcrystalline domains of ~25-32 nm by Scherrer. This is internally consistent for the bulk of the peaks and represents the robust size result. However, the full per-peak Scherrer set spans 7 to 140 nm (>3x, in fact ~20x), which by the domain rules indicates anisotropic broadening and/or unreliable widths for a few peaks: the two ~140 nm and ~139 nm values come from very narrow (~0.057 deg) low-index peaks near 5 deg that sit on the residual tail of the excluded air-scatter climb and are likely contaminated/instrument-limited, while the ~7 nm value (peak_13, FWHM 1.1 deg) is a broad, low-amplitude feature likely absorbing background curvature or overlap rather than a real nanocrystalline domain. The Williamson-Hall analysis is diagnostic: R^2 = 0.012 (essentially zero correlation), so the size-strain decomposition (nominal 30 nm, strain 0.0011) must NOT be reported as physical - the linear W-H model does not apply, consistent with anisotropic/mixed broadening. Several eta values pinned at exactly 0 (pure Lorentzian) or 1 (pure Gaussian) show the mixing is unconstrained for those peaks. The apex residuals at 11.9 and 20 deg are the expected profile limit for the sharpest cores and, given this is an in-situ dehydration ramp, may also carry a small physical signature of evolving strain/lattice contraction; they do not indicate a missing reflection. Because this is a single frame of a variable-temperature series, position shifts across frames (lattice change) are expected and are the point of the experiment. The most defensible quantitative outcome is a microcrystalline domain size of ~30 nm (2 sig figs) from the well-behaved mid-angle reflections, reported as a range (~25-32 nm) and explicitly flagged as anisotropic, with the outlier peaks excluded.

5. Caveats & Limitations

Model and data are well matched, so caveats are physical/analytical rather than model-form. (1) The Williamson-Hall decomposition (nominal 30 nm, strain 0.0011) is NOT reportable: R^2 = 0.012 means the linear model fails - report size range only, not strain. (2) Per-peak Scherrer sizes span ~7-140 nm (>3x), signaling anisotropic broadening; the isotropic mean of 38 nm is an oversimplification and the ~140 nm low-angle outliers are likely instrument/air-scatter-tail contaminated and should be excluded, while the 7 nm broad feature likely absorbs background or overlap. (3) Sharp +/-8 sigma derivative residuals at the 11.9 and 20 deg apexes and max_abs_residual/noise ~24.5 are the irreducible split-pseudo-Voigt profile limit for the sharpest cores, not missing reflections - do not over-refine. (4) Several eta values are pinned at 0 or 1, so Gaussian-Lorentzian mixing is unconstrained for those peaks. (5) This is one frame of a variable-temperature series; absolute positions/lattice will shift frame-to-frame by design, so single-frame lattice/size values are snapshots, not equilibrium values. (6) The phase itself has not been identified - all claims are morphological, not structural.
