The linear baseline and 0.206 baseline fraction confirm a non-fluorescent crystalline oxide. The persistent bimodal residual on the 141 cm-1 band suggests real asymmetry (Fano-type or anharmonic broadening common in the anatase Eg mode) that a symmetric pseudo-Voigt cannot fully capture. No chemistry (EDX/XRF) was provided, so this is a pattern-only identification, but the full five-band match (positions AND relative intensities) strongly supports anatase over any single-band coincidence.

5. Caveats & Limitations

No elemental chemistry (EDX/XRF) was provided, so identification is pattern-based (though all five diagnostic bands match in position and intensity). The strongest band (~141 cm-1) shows a bimodal/dispersive residual well above noise, indicating real lineshape asymmetry that the symmetric pseudo-Voigt under-fits; its fitted FWHM and eta (railed at 1.0) are therefore approximate. The positive linear-baseline slope over-extends beyond ~900 cm-1 where no real intensity exists. Peak_3 (~297, broad) and peak_7 (~794, FWHM railed at 50 cm-1) are weakly constrained (large fractional errors, eta on rails) and may partly model background or be second-order features rather than fundamental modes; they should not be over-interpreted as distinct phases. R2 = 0.987 alone does not validate the fit given these structured residuals.
