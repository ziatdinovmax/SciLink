Interpretation

The edge is unambiguously an Fe L2,3 ionization edge. Peak positions (L3 ~710.5 eV, L2 ~723.9 eV) and the spin-orbit splitting of ~13.4 eV are consistent with Fe in an oxidized state; the white-line energies and the presence of pronounced L3 fine structure (shoulder near 708.8 eV) are typical of Fe(III)/mixed-valence oxides where crystal-field and multiplet effects split the L3 manifold. The L3/L2 white-line intensity ratio is a well-known oxidation-state proxy for Fe, and the fit returns ~7.7 — but this value is not physically credible: for Fe oxides the L3/L2 ratio is typically ~3-5, and a value near 8 signals that the Lorentzian model has mis-partitioned white-line intensity into the continuum steps (the L2 step collapsed to zero) and mis-modeled the sharp L3 core. The residual diagnostics support this: the L3 region shows a systematic ±60 (>3 sigma) oscillation with sign changes on the peak (too-broad core, tail overshoot), a one-sided undershoot across the 713-716 eV post-edge tail, and slowly varying autocorrelated residuals from 713-730 eV indicating an imperfect continuum shape. Per the claim-anchored check, the 708.8 eV 'shoulder' is not a cleanly resolved component — it is adjacent to a large residual excess and its width is pinned at the fit bound — so it should be reported as unresolved L3 fine-structure / lineshape excess rather than a quantified separate peak. The physically robust output is therefore limited to the identification and coarse energetics; oxidation-state quantification via L3/L2 ratio should be redone with Voigt/pseudo-Voigt profiles and a properly constrained continuum before being reported.
